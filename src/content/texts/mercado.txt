# How to write a story
#
# Two parts, in this order: "## Historia", "## Retelling".
# Paragraphs are separated by a blank line. Spanish first, then its
# translation on lines starting with ">".
#
# [le gusta]        groups words into one term (it must be in lexicon.txt)
# [mañana:morning]  uses a specific sense of a word or phrase
#
# The retelling must line up with the story: same paragraphs, same
# number of sentences in each, so sentences can be shown side by side.
#
# Each paragraph ends with its reading question, answered while reading
# and checked on the results step:
#
#   Q: ¿Qué compra el abuelo?         the question, then "> " translation
#   A: Naranjas.                       a model answer, then "> " translation
#   accept: unas naranjas | naranja    other correct answers (beginner levels)
#
# Beginner (A) answers are a word or two: a noun with its article, an
# adjective, an activity. Intermediate (B) answers are one short sentence
# about something the paragraph implies but doesn't say, so it can't be
# copied; most change the person, about a third also change the tense to
# one already seen in the text. In the retelling, the narrator asks in the
# first person ("¿Qué llevo hoy?") and the learner answers with tú.
#
# Lines starting with # are comments.

## Historia

[Todos los sábados], Lucía va al mercado con su abuelo. El mercado está en una plaza pequeña, [cerca del] río. Hay muchos puestos de frutas, verduras, pan y flores.
> Every Saturday, Lucía goes to the market with her grandfather. The market is in a small square, near the river. There are many stalls of fruit, vegetables, bread and flowers.
Q: ¿Con quién va Lucía al mercado?
> Who does Lucía go to the market with?
A: Con su abuelo.
> With her grandfather.
accept: su abuelo | el abuelo | con el abuelo

Hoy [hace frío], pero el sol brilla. Lucía lleva una bufanda roja. Su abuelo quiere comprar naranjas, porque mañana por la [mañana:morning] [va a] preparar un pastel para su vecina.
> Today it's cold, but the sun is shining. Lucía is wearing a red scarf. Her grandfather wants to buy oranges, because tomorrow morning he is going to make a cake for his neighbor.
Q: ¿Qué va a preparar el abuelo?
> What is the grandfather going to make?
A: Un pastel.
> A cake.
accept: pastel

En el primer puesto, las naranjas son caras. Cuestan tres euros el kilo. [Por eso], Lucía y su abuelo van al segundo puesto. Allí, las naranjas cuestan dos euros el kilo. Compran dos kilos. Además, la vendedora le regala un limón a Lucía.
> At the first stall, the oranges are expensive. They cost three euros a kilo. That's why Lucía and her grandfather go to the second stall. There, the oranges cost two euros a kilo. They buy two kilos. On top of that, the seller gives Lucía a lemon.
Q: ¿Cómo son las naranjas en el primer puesto?
> What are the oranges like at the first stall?
A: Caras.
> Expensive.
accept: son caras | muy caras

Después, compran pan y unas flores amarillas. [Más tarde], vuelven a casa. Lucía está contenta. A Lucía [le gusta] mucho el mercado.
> Afterward, they buy bread and some yellow flowers. Later, they go back home. Lucía is happy. Lucía really likes the market.
Q: ¿Qué compran después de las naranjas?
> What do they buy after the oranges?
A: Pan y flores.
> Bread and flowers.
accept: pan y unas flores | flores y pan | unas flores y pan
## Retelling

[Todos los sábados], voy al mercado con mi abuelo. El mercado está en una plaza pequeña, [cerca del] río. Hay muchos puestos de frutas, verduras, pan y flores.
> Every Saturday, I go to the market with my grandfather. The market is in a small square, near the river. There are many stalls of fruit, vegetables, bread and flowers.
Q: ¿Qué día de la semana voy al mercado?
> What day of the week do I go to the market?
A: Los sábados.
> On Saturdays.
accept: el sábado | sábados | sábado | todos los sábados

Hoy [hace frío], pero el sol brilla. Llevo una bufanda roja. Mi abuelo quiere comprar naranjas, porque mañana por la [mañana:morning] [va a] preparar un pastel para su vecina.
> Today it's cold, but the sun is shining. I'm wearing a red scarf. My grandfather wants to buy oranges, because tomorrow morning he is going to make a cake for his neighbor.
Q: ¿Qué llevo hoy?
> What am I wearing today?
A: Una bufanda roja.
> A red scarf.
accept: una bufanda | llevas una bufanda roja

En el primer puesto, las naranjas son caras. Cuestan tres euros el kilo. [Por eso], mi abuelo y yo vamos al segundo puesto. Allí, las naranjas cuestan dos euros el kilo. Compramos dos kilos. Además, la vendedora me regala un limón.
> At the first stall, the oranges are expensive. They cost three euros a kilo. That's why my grandfather and I go to the second stall. There, the oranges cost two euros a kilo. We buy two kilos. On top of that, the seller gives me a lemon.
Q: ¿Qué me regala la vendedora?
> What does the seller give me?
A: Un limón.
> A lemon.
accept: limón | te regala un limón

Después, compramos pan y unas flores amarillas. [Más tarde], volvemos a casa. Estoy contenta. [Me gusta] mucho el mercado.
> Afterward, we buy bread and some yellow flowers. Later, we go back home. I'm happy. I really like the market.
Q: ¿Cómo estoy al final del día?
> How am I at the end of the day?
A: Contenta.
> Happy.
accept: estás contenta | muy contenta | feliz | estás feliz
